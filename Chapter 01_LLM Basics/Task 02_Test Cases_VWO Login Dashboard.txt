4. Important Anti-Hallucination Observation

Notice something important in the test cases above.

For example, I did not write:

"Enter an invalid email such as abc@ and verify the error message Invalid email address."

Why?

Because the PRD only says that email format validation exists. It does not provide the exact invalid-data rules or error message.

So the safe test case is:

Validate email format → move focus away → verify email format validation is triggered.

This is exactly the kind of difference your Anti-Hallucination rules are designed to control.

5. What happens WITHOUT Anti-Hallucination Rules?

This is where the difference becomes very important for QA.

With Anti-Hallucination
PRD
 ↓
Extract verified facts
 ↓
Identify missing information
 ↓
Create TC only from verified facts
 ↓
Self-validation
 ↓
Reliable TC
Without Anti-Hallucination

An AI may try to make the test cases look more complete by filling gaps with assumptions.

For example:

PRD says	Hallucinated TC might say
Email validation exists	"Enter abc@ and verify Invalid email address"
Rate limiting exists	"After 5 failed attempts, account is locked for 15 minutes"
Session timeout is configurable	"User is logged out after 30 minutes"
Password complexity exists	"Password must contain 8 characters, one uppercase, one number and one special character"
2FA is optional	"Enter 6-digit OTP received by SMS"
SSO supports SAML/OAuth	"Click Login with SAML button and enter corporate credentials"
Dashboard transition exists	"Verify URL is /dashboard"

None of those exact details are present in the PRD.

So they would violate your anti-hallucination rule:

DO NOT invent features, APIs, error codes, UI elements, or behavior.
